# Full screen is the green light, and there is one desktop

2026-09-19. Tanmai, after a day with many desktops: "can we get rid of the
multiple desktops thing for now, I think that's really confusing", and
"fill screen and fullscreen are different, and the fullscreen should only
activate if we click the green button, and we want to hide the menu bar
in fullscreen too, we want to just show the window with the title bar of
the window."

## One desktop

Every window lies on the one desktop. Desktops a window could be moved
between, a filled window being a desktop of its own, the Window menu's
list of them and their keys were built on 2026-09-18 and taken out the
next day. A migration takes `desk` and `home` off every saved window, and
a second gathers the windows of any desktop row past a person's first
onto that first one before the others go, so none is out of reach.

## Two things, not one

Filling makes a window as big as the desktop: under the menu bar, up to
the dock. It is a double-click on the title bar, Control-Option-Return,
or "Fill the screen" in the window's menu, and it is `full` on the card,
kept with the desktop, so the window is filled on every device.

Full screen is the green light's alone, on a laptop. The window is the
whole display with its own bar and nothing else: the menu bar and the
dock are not drawn, the stage takes the whole viewport, and the browser's
own full screen is asked for with it. It is the device's own and is kept
nowhere. The window is drawn over every other while it stands. Opening
or raising another window from this device ends it, rather than hiding
the new window behind it; Escape, the light again and the browser
leaving full screen end it too; a window raised on another device does
not. Its bar answers a double-click and its lights and carries it
nowhere, the keys that snap or fill do nothing under it, and
notifications still arrive over it. The window comes back as it was,
filled if it was filled.

One window is filled at a time: filling one lets any other down, and two
filled on two devices meet as one, the one in front.

A phone has no full screen to give, held either way up, so its green
light fills, as it did.
