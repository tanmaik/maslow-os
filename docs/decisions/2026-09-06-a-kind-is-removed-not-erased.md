# 2026-09-06 — a kind is removed, not erased

A kind or a verb leaves the vocabulary the way a record leaves the brain:
hidden and kept, and brought back by restore. Nothing in the brain is
erased by a door; the log is the record of what was, and a removal is one
more line in it.

## What removal means

Undefining a kind is refused while a live record is of it, and a verb while
an edge carries it: change or remove those first. Records already removed
do not stand in the way; they go quiet with their kind and come back after
it. A removed kind leaves the catalog and the handshake, its fields with
it, and stops accepting records. Its name stays its own while it is away,
so a write that names it is told it is removed and to restore it or choose
another name, and restoring never collides with a kind defined since.

Restore takes kinds and verbs by name beside records by id. A record comes
back only once its kind is there, and says so otherwise.

## Why

The first live test left two throwaway kinds in a real catalog that could
not leave, because every record of them, removed or not, held them in place.
Erasing records for good would have been the other way out, and it is the
wrong one for a brain: the log is the person's memory of their own mind,
and a purge would make it lie. Hiding the kind instead keeps every promise
the brain already makes about records, and gives the catalog back.

## What it does not do

A field is still taken off a kind outright, and its values out of the
records, since a field's history is in the log and it declares nothing that
a record depends on to exist. Shares on a removed kind stay, so it comes
back shared as it was.
