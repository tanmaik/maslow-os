# 2026-09-14 — the agent arranges the desktop

The desktop is the ground under the windows, like the desktop under a Mac's
windows, and the agent arranges it. Three tools on the brain's MCP, beside
`notify` and `ask`: `desktop` says what lies there, `place` puts a widget
down or moves one, `unplace` takes one off. Windows are the person's and
the agent never sees them.

## Why

What the agent could put in front of the person was a note in the bar. Tanishk, 2026-09-14: things
should "just automatically pop up on my screen if they're deemed
important", and "widgets should look like widgets instead of windows".
And: "the desktop shouldn't care about what windows are open, you know? it
should be like how a desktop works on a computer, just placing it on the
desktop."

## What it is

- **A widget, never a window, and a widget is an app on a port.** `place`
  makes a widget: an app served on a port of the person's computer, which
  the agent builds and runs there, or a port a colleague opened to them,
  lying flat under every window with no bar, at a position and size as
  shares of the desktop, or where the next widget goes. Windows come from the
  person's clicks and from nothing else. Records and pages were widgets
  for an afternoon and were taken back the same day: a record's page on
  the desktop is a document squeezed into a frame, "kind of ugly and very
  wordy" (Tanishk), and what people will want on a desktop is a surface made
  for the glance, which the agent has to build. A note in the bar still
  points at records.
- **The ground, not the air.** `desktop` lists widgets and nothing of the
  windows. The agent arranges around what it placed, the person arranges
  what they opened, and a widget under a window is a widget under a
  window.
- **An ordinary card.** What the agent places is the same card a person
  makes from the dock's "Put on the desktop": dragged, resized, put away,
  taken off or made a window from its grip. Nothing is marked as the
  agent's and nothing is reserved for it.
- **Position and size, not slots.** The most important thing goes at the
  top because the agent puts it there, and the desktop stays uncluttered
  because the agent takes things off. A fixed slot would be a layout we
  wrote.
- **The desktop follows the server.** Each desktop carries a count of how many
  times it was kept. A save from the page names the count it saw; one
  that fell behind is refused, the desktop as it now is comes back, and the
  page takes it in with its own changes kept over it and its own unsaved
  cards kept. The five-second ask the room already makes after ports now
  says the count, so a desktop kept elsewhere, by the agent most often, is
  on screen within seconds on every device. Same rule as a record's save.
- **The brief says what each surface is for, and no more.** The desktop is
  where the person looks, and a widget stays on it on every device until
  taken off; the bar is for what is read once and cleared, or answered.
  A widget is an app served on a port of the computer, so the agent
  builds what it wants seen and places the port. What deserves the desktop, and
  where, and how many, is the agent's judgment; a person who wants it
  otherwise says so in their own `CLAUDE.md` on their computer. The first
  runs on the preview left only notes, because the brief listed the two
  surfaces as equals and said nothing of which was which.

## What it is not

- Not a wake. Placing happens inside runs the clock, a share or an
  answered ask already start.
- Not a table. Widgets live in the desktop's one saved arrangement, as they
  did.
- Not a phone thing. A widget was never drawn on a phone and still is not.
