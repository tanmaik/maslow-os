# 2026-09-04 — sharing inside an org

The founders use one brain as a team, and an enterprise customer needs an
org where not everyone knows everything. Both need the same thing: a record
stays its writer's, and its writer decides who else may see it, change it, or
do everything with it.

## Ownership is not visibility

Ownership is who wrote the record. It never transfers and always grants
owner level. Visibility widens only through grants: a record, a subject, a
level, who gave it. A grant's subject is one member, one group, or everyone.
Everyone is every current member of the org, computed, never stored, never
edited. Ownerless, org-level records were considered and rejected: they would
have been the only way to share and every one would need reclassifying when
finer rules arrived.

## Highest level wins, no deny rules

A member's level on a record is the most any path gives them: they own it, a
grant names them, or a grant names a group they are in. Deny rules make "why
can't I see this" unanswerable, so there are none.

## The four calls

Org owners manage groups, not content. Editors may not share, so who can see
a record is always "the owner decided". The agent's conclusions default to
private. Everyone can only be given view.

## How the database does it

`grant_level(record)` is a function that reads grants as their owner (row
security is enabled on grants and not forced, and only the app role connects
at request time), so the policy on records can call it without asking the
grants policy and without recursion. A grant names its member or group by a
same-org key, and a deleted group or membership takes its grants with it. Records: seen by their owner or at
grant level one, changed at two, written by their owner. Edges: seen when
both ends are, made by an editor of the from-end who can see the to-end. A
change is seen by whoever can see what changed. Remove, restore and merge are
owner acts enforced in the doors, since the database cannot tell one update
from another.

## Not yet

Nested groups. Ownership transfer. Sharing across orgs. Link or time-limited
sharing. Kind-level defaults. Group-level managers. The org's own agent as a
membership. Logging of shares in the event log: a share would be an
org-visible event and would say too much.
